===== SLIDE 2 =====
  Overview
  Abstract
  Problem
  Serverless computing provisions resources on demand, but cold starts spike latency while idle warm containers waste energy.
  Data & Forecast
  Azure Functions traces are preprocessed into time-series sequences; a Temporal Fusion Transformer predicts short-term workload.
  Agentic Planning
  Qwen3 interprets predicted demand, system state and SLA status to produce a high-level container-management plan.
  RL Control
  A PPO controller executes fast decisions — NoOp, PreWarm, ScaleOut, Reuse, ScaleIn or Terminate — with SLA as the primary constraint.
  Evaluation
  The closed-loop system is compared against reactive autoscaling on latency, SLA compliance, cold starts, energy and utilisation.

===== SLIDE 3 =====
  Problem Statement
  The Challenge with Serverless Scaling
  Serverless workloads vary rapidly. Reactive scaling responds
  after
  demand changes, causing cold-start latency spikes. Keeping containers warm reduces cold starts but inflates idle energy and resource waste.
  Core Problem:
  Anticipate workload changes and autonomously manage containers so energy is minimised without violating SLA requirements.
  ⏱
  Cold-Start Delay
  New containers require initialisation, adding response latency during demand spikes.
  ⚡
  Idle Energy Waste
  Warm containers kept on standby consume power even when receiving zero invocations.
  📉
  Reactive Limitation
  Threshold-based autoscalers scale too late; sudden spikes breach SLA before action is taken.
  🔢
  Optimisation Target
  Minimise
  E + αL + βCS + γC
  subject to P95 latency ≤ SLA target.

===== SLIDE 4 =====
  Introduction
  Context & Motivation
  Serverless Growth
  Event-driven applications create highly variable, burst-prone workloads that challenge traditional provisioning.
  Cold-Start Challenge
  A new container must initialise its runtime before serving requests, directly increasing end-user response latency.
  Reactive Scaling Limit
  Scaling triggered by observed demand arrives too late for sudden spikes, causing SLA breaches at the moment of peak load.
  Energy Efficiency Need
  Excessive warm capacity consumes significant energy during low-demand periods, raising operational cost and carbon footprint.
  Predictive Solution
  Combining Transformer forecasting, Qwen3 agentic planning and PPO reinforcement learning enables proactive, autonomous control.

===== SLIDE 5 =====
  Objectives
  Project Objectives & Targets
  Workload Forecasting
  TFT to predict short-term invocation demand from multivariate workload and runtime features.
  SLA Protection
  Strongly prioritize P95 latency within the defined SLA target; violations carry the highest penalty.
  Proactive Scaling
  Prewarm or scale containers before demand arrives, eliminating reactive cold-start latency.
  Energy Optimization
  Reduce unnecessary active and idle container time to lower overall energy consumption.
  Agentic Planning via Qwen3
  High-level reasoning over forecast, system state and SLA to generate orchestration plans.
  Rigorous Evaluation
  Compare against reactive autoscaling across multiple workload patterns with clear metrics.
